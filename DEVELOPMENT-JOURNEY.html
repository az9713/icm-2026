1. The brief, and what it actually asked

The session opened with a question, not a task:

"are there any known, outstanding problems with the *md summaries created, if any"

Under this account's standing rule, a question is read-only. So the first answer was a report, not an edit. That report surfaced one dominant problem, and the follow-up was:

"how can the one big problem be fixed" … then … "ok finish all the tiers"

The scope therefore grew in three steps: audit, then plan, then execute. That ordering mattered. Had the session started at "fix the tutorials", it would have started editing files whose defects were still unknown.

Invisible constraints that shaped everything

A reader of the output cannot see these, so they belong here.

  • ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English was active from the first reply. Short active sentences, one meaning per word, no idiom. It changes sentences only; every identifier, number and file path survives intact.
  • Ponytail mode (full) was active: the laziest solution that works, standard library before custom code, shortest working diff, delete before add. This is why the citation check is one 60-line script and not a framework.
  • A standing project rule from CLAUDE.md: "Finish, or say what you left." It is why every reply in this session carried an explicit not-done line.
  • A standing repo rule from HANDOFF.md: verify a push by comparing git rev-parse --short HEAD against git ls-remote origin main, never by trusting a clean exit.

2. How the tutorials were written (reconstructed)

Evidence: TEMPLATE.md (16 KB, committed in 58a9001), the git log, and HANDOFF.md.

The reader is one named person

TEMPLATE.md §1 opens by fixing the audience precisely, and this decision drives the rest of the spec. The reader has real and complex analysis, ODEs and PDEs, linear algebra, probability, Fourier analysis, numerical methods, calculus of variations, classical and statistical mechanics, optimization, some functional analysis. The reader does not have algebraic geometry, algebraic topology, number theory, representation theory, category theory, geometric group theory, low-dimensional topology, or derived algebraic geometry.

That split explains the whole difficulty-rating scheme, and it explains why the Lurie and Gaitsgory tutorials look so different from the Wright and Bartlett ones.

The pipeline

StepWhat happened
1. SourceYouTube auto-captions for each of 20 ICM 2026 plenary lectures, downloaded and cleaned into transcripts/{videoId}_transcript.txt. The download and clean commands live in TEMPLATE.md §4, deliberately, so they are not session-transient.
2. Companion huntAn arXiv, Crossref, Semantic Scholar and OpenAlex sweep per speaker. Cost about 284,000 tokens. HANDOFF.md records the instruction not to re-run it.
3. PilotTwo tutorials written by hand in-session (Kontorovich d174da8, Wright 96c1a92), then three pilots. The pilots produced the spec rules.
4. Fan-out15 tutorials written by concurrent subagents against TEMPLATE.md, landing in 58a9001.
5. CompletionBrendle, Dolgopyat and Mok in e0e5f72; a Mok self-revision in aea8545.

The three rules that made concurrency survivable

Recorded in the project memory file, and they held:

  1. Agents write only their own two files. Never sources.txt, never any shared index, never git. Concurrent appends corrupt a shared file; concurrent commits collide on .git/index.lock.
  2. The instructions live in a spec file the agents read, not in the prompt. Eighteen prompts produce eighteen different documents. One TEMPLATE.md produces one set.
  3. Pilot three before spending eighteen.

The hard rule that everything else rests on

Never invent mathematics. Auto-captions carry no formulas — the mathematics lived on the board and the slides, invisible to the caption track. If the transcript and the companion document together do not support a statement, do not write it. …A tutorial with honest holes is useful. A tutorial with plausible fabrications is poison — the reader cannot tell which parts to trust, so they must distrust all of it.
— TEMPLATE.md §5

Hold on to the first clause. Auto-captions carry no formulas. It is the single most important structural fact in this entire project, and section 8 returns to it.

The writing agents were each supposed to file a report on what they had reconstructed and what they could not verify. Fifteen of them hit an account-level session limit and died. They died holding their reports in their final message, so the reports were destroyed with them.

The files themselves survived. Only the assurance was lost. That produced the state HANDOFF.md recorded before this session:

4. The core problem: what "verified" was allowed to mean

This is the crux of the session, and it is worth stating carefully, because the whole verification design follows from it.

There are at least four different things "verified" could mean for a tutorial built from auto-captions:

LevelQuestion it answersCan an agent do it?
StructuralDoes the file parse, and are the sections present?Yes, trivially.
Citation existenceDo the cited papers exist?Yes, mechanically, via APIs.
ProvenanceDoes every claim carry a source — the transcript, or a citation?Yes, by reading both.
TruthIs the mathematics correct?No — not without reading and understanding every cited paper.

The decision made in this session was to buy the third level and be loud about not buying the fourth. The verifier brief says it in as many words:

You cannot check whether the mathematics is TRUE; you do not have the papers. You check whether each claim has a source.
— verify/BRIEF.md

That was the right trade for the budget. But it has a consequence that section 10 develops: a "CLEAN" verdict on this scale is not a statement that the mathematics is right.

5. The three-tier check, and why it was built that way

The design principle was cheapest signal first: do not spend 500,000 tokens to learn something a two-minute script can tell you.

TierWhat it doesCostCatches
0A script resolves every arXiv ID and DOI against the arXiv and Crossref APIs. Reads no mathematics.~2 min, no tokensFabricated citations only.
1Read only each file's final "Note on the tutorial process", where the writing agent self-reported its gaps.~26k tokensSelf-declared weakness.
2Full read of any file that tiers 0 or 1 flagged.VariableTargeted depth.
3One independent verifier per tutorial, reading the full tutorial against the full transcript.~520k input tokensProvenance defects.

Tiers 1 and 2 were folded into tier 3, deliberately. Each verifier was told to read the writing agent's own process note and judge whether that self-report was honest and complete. Running tier 1 separately would have meant reading the same text twice. This decision was stated to the user before execution and is recorded in verify/README.md.

The durability fix

The original wave died holding its reports. So the brief made the report an artifact, not a message:

Create verify/{SLUG}.md after you finish reading the FIRST major section, then APPEND to it as you read on. Do not hold the report in memory until the end. If your session dies, a partial file on disk is the whole point.

Plus a .DONE marker per file, so a restart skips finished work, and a batch size of five rather than fifteen, because the limit that killed the writers was account-level. All 15 reports landed. Mid-run, five in-progress reports were visible on disk with no .DONE marker — the design working as intended.

Fifteen of fifteen verifiers list this first under "What I could not check". Representative quotes, verbatim from the reports:

7.2 The companion papers — in most files

This is the finding I did not expect, and it is the most consequential thing in this document. Only 6 of the 15 verifiers actually opened a companion paper and compared statements against it. The other 9 checked the tutorial against the transcript and the citation list alone.

The paper-fetch instruction was not in the shared brief. It appeared in the per-agent prompt, and I only started writing it after the first batch had already launched. The five files that received an explicit "fetch it, try ar5iv" instruction — gerard, oh, buffa, tolsa, morris — were the last five launched. brendle fetched on its own initiative.

Meanwhile batch 1 — lurie, dolgopyat, sarnak, tardos, mok — was chosen precisely because HANDOFF.md ranked those files highest-risk. They received the shallowest check. Check depth ran exactly opposite to assessed risk.

Four Grade B files remain unchecked at this depth: manolescu and mok have no reachable companion, sarnak's is Serre's French Bourbaki text, and lurie has none.

8. Mathematical formulae: the real implications

Start from the structural fact in TEMPLATE.md §5:

Auto-captions carry no formulas. The mathematics lived on the board and the slides, invisible to the caption track.

Read that again with the verification result in hand. It means:

Not one formula in any of the 20 tutorials came from the talk. Every formula was either restored from a paper, or supplied by the writing agent. The transcript — which is the one source every verifier read — is structurally incapable of confirming a single equation.

So formula fidelity depends entirely on the paper-restoration step. And that step was checked in 6 files out of 15.

The new MAJOR, exactly

SourceStatement
Remenik, eq (4.2)K_t = lim e^{−(1/3)t∂³ + ℓ₁∂²} P^{Hit h} e^{(1/3)t∂³ − ℓ₂∂²}

An operator became a scalar, and the time variable vanished from the right-hand side of a definition whose left-hand side is K_t. The line is labelled "transcribed", and it is the formula from which §4.5's decoupling punchline is read off.

8.2 The two MAJOR errors, exactly

Both are in summaries/hardy-spaces-explicit-formulae-gerard.md, and both carry equation-number attributions, which is what makes them dangerous — they look sourced.

Error 1 — :665, the distorted Plancherel identity

SourceStatement
Paper, eq (2.7)Σ|⟨f,φ_j⟩|² + ∫₀^∞|f̃^±|² dλ/(2π) = ∫|f|²
TutorialΣ|⟨f,φ_j⟩|² + ∫₀^∞|f̃^±|² dλ = 2π∫|f|²

The 1/(2π) was moved off the integral and onto the right-hand side — but the discrete sum was not rescaled with it. The verifier closed the obvious escape route: the paper's own definition (2.5) of f̃^± carries no prefactor and is character-identical to the tutorial's, so no alternative normalisation rescues it.

Error 2 — :399, the Lax operator

The tutorial writes B_u = i(T_{|D|u} − T_{u²}). Both arXiv:2212.03139 and the transcript say T_u² — the square of the Toeplitz operator, not the Toeplitz operator of u². The transcript is explicit: "minus the square of TU". This tutorial contradicts both of its own sources at once.

8.3 The dominant error class is a dropped qualifier

Look at the shape of all four formula defects found:

  • gerard:665 — a factor moved without rescaling a companion term.
  • gerard:399 — a subscript scope changed (T_{u²} vs T_u²).
  • buffa:465-467 — companion eq (33) quoted with its 1/h(γ_jl) weight dropped, inside a gap block that calls the scaling unrecoverable. The equation it cites in the same sentence contains the answer.
  • buffa:910,:916 — the tutorial's own exercise integrates ρ as a/δ after defining it as 1 + a/δ. Correct slope δ(1+a)/(δ+a), not δ(1+a)/a.

Round 2 added five more, and every one of them is the same shape:

  • quastel:481 — t∂³ replaced by ℓ₁³ and ℓ₂³.
  • yau:964 — a constant given as 2 Im m_sc(E)/√(4−E²), which is identically 1; the answer is its reciprocal, 1/Im m_sc(E).
  • yau:963 — a dropped minus sign, contradicting the expression printed beside it.
  • mok:1461 — an exponent one too high, contradicted by the expansion above it and by part (c) below it.
  • brendle:1252 — a metric and its curvature that differ by a factor of 4.

None of these is a hallucinated theorem. Every one is a real formula with a small piece altered or lost. This class is invisible to every check except character-level comparison against the source — or, for the last four, against the rest of the same paragraph. A citation check passes it. A plausibility read passes it. A reader who trusts the equation number passes it.

The second rule this earns

Four of the nine defects round 2 found are contradicted somewhere else in the same solution. The cheapest check in this whole project is one nobody ran: read each passage against itself. It needs no paper, no transcript and no network.

The rule this earns

A formula with an equation number is more dangerous than a formula without one, because the number transfers the paper's authority to a string the paper never contained.

8.4 Notation is not uniform, and nobody specified it

TEMPLATE.md never says how to write mathematics. The result, measured across the corpus:

StyleFiles
Zero LaTeX — all mathematics in Unicode proseziegler, manolescu, gaitsgory, oh, lurie, mok (6)
Zero Unicode — all mathematics in LaTeXgerard (51 display, 524 inline), buffa (29 display, 262 inline) (2)

Corpus totals: 367 display blocks ($$), 1,369 inline $…$ spans, and 3,985 Unicode mathematical characters.

Correction, round 2. This paragraph first printed 1,048 inline spans. Two independent recounts disagree with that and with each other in an informative way: a whole-file delimiter pairing gives 1,369, and a per-line regex that only matches spans opening and closing on one line gives 1,338. The gap of 31 is real — the files hard-wrap at about 90 characters, so some inline maths straddles a newline. 1,369 is the figure to use; the original 1,048 was an undercount by some method not recorded. The display-block count, 367, is confirmed by both. The 3,985 Unicode figure was not re-derived.

Two consequences.

Rendering. If these files are ever rendered to HTML without MathJax or KaTeX — which is exactly what the "optional dark-mode HTML render" idea in HANDOFF.md would do — then 1,369 inline spans and 367 display blocks turn into literal dollar signs, while the Unicode files render perfectly. Nobody has checked this, because nobody has rendered them.

Falsifiability. This one is subtler and it is the part worth sitting with. The two files with the heaviest LaTeX are exactly the two files where formula errors were found. The file that scored CLEAN, mok, contains zero LaTeX and zero displayed equations — its mathematics is carried in quoted prose and Unicode symbols inside blockquotes.

Stated as an inference, not a fact

A tutorial that expresses mathematics as prose has fewer falsifiable objects in it. It cannot fail a formula check, because it contains no formula to check. Its CLEAN verdict is therefore a weaker signal than the same verdict on a formula-dense file.

This is an inference from the notation counts and the verdict distribution. It is not something a verifier reported. It should be tested, not assumed — see the actions in section 10.

9. What went wrong in this session

The failures here are mine, and several were caught only by review.

9.1 Thirty-five false positives from one bad heuristic

The first citation check compared each citation's title against the line that cited it. It raised 23 author/title mismatches and 12 date mismatches. All 35 were defects in my script.

  • The 23 were bare ID references in prose, with the full citation elsewhere in the file.
  • The 12 were tutorials correctly giving both a v1 and a v2 date, where my parser compared v1 only. One was a Bourbaki seminar delivery date, correctly distinguished from the arXiv date.

The tutorials were more careful than the tool auditing them. The title-matching heuristic was deleted; only resolution was kept. verify/check_citations.py carries a # ponytail: comment recording why.

9.2 I reported an error in the Dolgopyat file that was not an error

Tier 0 showed the Crossref chapter title as "Randomness, Rotations, Renormalization, and Resonances" while the front matter said "Randomness, Rotations, and Resonances". I reported a dropped word. Line 47 of that file already gives the four-R chapter title and explains that the talk was announced with three. The tutorial had done the work; I had not read far enough before reporting.

What survived: a genuine small inconsistency in the paper: field, which said "none retrievable" without naming the DOI that Crossref does hold. Fixed.

9.3 A cross-file claim I published and then had to retract

I wrote in verify/CROSS-FILE.md that invented timing was "confined to one file". My grep only matched HH:MM patterns. The Morris verifier then reported two prose clock figures. A second sweep found about 20 such claims across 11 files. Corrected — with two caveats now recorded, because a stated total duration may legitimately come from yt-dlp metadata, and reader-facing exercise estimates like "(25 minutes)" are not this defect at all.

9.4 An overstatement in the roll-up, caught on review

verify/README.md and HANDOFF.md both said "all 20 gap-honesty checks pass". Two errors in one sentence: only 15 files were checked, and gap honesty was 14 PASS with 1 CONCERN. Corrected in d252cdf. The pushed commit message of f0dcda4 still carries the overstatement and cannot be edited; the files are what a later session reads.

9.5 The verifier brief nearly died in a scratchpad

VERIFY_BRIEF.md — the spec all 15 verifiers ran against — was written to the session scratchpad. That directory dies with the session. Re-verifying a file after a fix would have had no spec to run against. Copied to verify/BRIEF.md in d252cdf. This is the same mistake the Lurie March-2025 transcript made, one project earlier.

9.6 The scheduling error described in 7.2

I added the paper-fetch instruction to per-agent prompts as the run progressed, so the highest-risk files got the shallowest check. Nothing caught this during the run. It surfaced only when I read all 15 "What I could not check" sections together while writing this document.

10. Unknown unknowns

These are the things not on anyone's list — not in HANDOFF.md, not in any verifier report, not in the question that opened this session. Each carries what to do about it.

UU-1. The five "verified" files are the least verified in the corpus

The mental model carried into this session was: 5 verified, 15 unknown. That ranking is inverted. The 15 received an independent read by an agent that did not write them. The 5 were verified by the same session that wrote them — Kontorovich and Wright were hand-written and then self-checked. Self-verification is the weakest form of verification there is, and the label "verified" hides it.

Nobody re-checked those 5 in this run. They have had no title check, no gap-honesty check, no independent reader.

Done in round 2. All five — kontorovich, wright, otto, bartlett, gaitsgory — now have an independent report in verify/, written to the same brief. Title check 5 of 5 PASS, so the corpus is now 20 of 20. Verdicts: five MINOR, no MAJOR. Seven new provenance items, the sharpest being kontorovich:585 ("5 to 10 years" attributed to the speaker; not in the transcript) and kontorovich:559 (the LANA paragraph names two universities the talk does not, and dates the report a month before the speaker's "last week"). Done without subagents, in the main loop.

UU-2. Absence of found errors is not absence of errors — and the split is measurable

Formula errors were found in 2 of the 6 files where formulas were actually compared against a paper. Zero were found in the 9 files where no paper was opened — because nobody looked.

If the Grade A rate carried over, roughly 3 of the 9 Grade B files would contain an undetected formula defect. That is an extrapolation from a sample of six, not a measurement. It could be lower; the two hits are both PDE/numerics files with dense displayed equations, and the Grade B set is more prose-heavy. It is not zero.

Done in round 2, in exactly that order. Result: 1 of 5, not the ~3 of 9 this box predicted. quastel has a MAJOR formula error; yau, ziegler, tardos and dolgopyat came back clean against their companions, including every theorem number. The prediction was directionally right — a defect was there — and quantitatively too pessimistic. Section 12 has the detail.

UU-3. Nobody has ever checked the captions against the audio

The entire verification chain treats the transcript as ground truth for "what the speaker said". The transcript is a machine caption track, and it demonstrably mangles language: "Mark HOA" for Marc Hoyois, "atoms operations" for Adams operations, "boner sphere" for homology sphere, "piggoa and lao" for Aggarwal–Lopatto.

A caption engine that loses proper nouns can also lose a "not", a "for all" where the speaker said "there exists", or a "necessary" where he said "sufficient". Those failures are invisible: they produce fluent, plausible English. One was already caught by accident — the Yau tutorial silently fixed a spoken "λ large" to "λ small", which means at least one caption inverted a meaning and the writing agent noticed.

Do: spot-check audio for the 10 to 20 passages that carry the most weight — the theorem statements each tutorial builds on. This is the only defect class where the fix requires listening.

UU-4. Verifier and writer are the same class of system

Both are language models reading the same sources. There is no independent ground truth anywhere in this pipeline. If a writing agent misread a paper's normalisation convention, a verifier reading the same paper can misread it the same way and return PASS.

The two gerard errors were caught because ar5iv gave exact source text to compare character by character. Where a verifier had only an abstract — dolgopyat explicitly says "no papers open beyond abstracts" — agreement between writer and verifier proves much less than it appears to.

Do: treat "verifier fetched the full text" as the quality gate, not "verifier returned PASS". verify/{slug}.md records which happened for every file.

UU-5. The verdict scale compresses very different files into "MINOR"

Thirteen files are MINOR. That single word covers brendle with 0 uncited external claims and manolescu with 21. It covers oh, whose one defect is that five first names came from outside the bibliography, and yau, which quotes a living person — Noga Alon — sourced to nothing but the word "Quanta".

A reader skimming the roll-up will read MINOR as a quality grade. It is not one. It means "no invented theorem and no fabricated citation", which is a floor, not a rating.

Do: when acting on the fix list, sort by the per-file counts in verify/README.md, never by the verdict word.

UU-6. Correct-when-written is not correct-now, and nothing refreshes these

arithmetic-patterns-ziegler.md:921 and :953 state that two inverse theorems are qualitative with no effective bounds. That status comes from a companion paper written in April 2014. Line :331 hedges it correctly as "in the companion's words"; the two later lines drop the hedge and assert it as present fact. Its verifier could not confirm whether the status changed, because the session's web-search budget was exhausted at 200 of 200 searches.

Seven of 20 talks have an arXiv companion. Several companions are surveys that stop years before the talk. Any tutorial restoring "the current state of the field" from an old survey inherits that survey's expiry date, silently.

Do: grep the corpus for present-tense field-status claims ("remains open", "no effective bounds", "the best known"), and date-stamp each against the source it came from.

UU-7. The mathematics may not render at all

1,369 inline $…$ spans and 367 $$ display blocks exist across the corpus, alongside 3,985 Unicode mathematical characters. Six files use no LaTeX whatsoever; two use nothing else. TEMPLATE.md never picked a convention.

GitHub's Markdown renderer handles $…$ only in certain contexts. A plain HTML export handles none of it. The dark-mode HTML render sitting in HANDOFF.md as an optional next step would silently produce two classes of file: six that look right and two that look broken.

Do: before any render, decide the convention and load MathJax in the template. One test render of gerard (524 inline spans) and mok (zero) settles it in two minutes.
Round 2: checked by counting instead of rendering, and it found a concrete defect that a render would have shown as 70 lines of garbage — shape-of-math-kontorovich.md:637 writes **$10,000** with a bare dollar sign. It is the file's only inline $, so MathJax pairs it with the display block at :709. Every other file has balanced delimiters; 367 display blocks all pair. Fix: escape it as \$10,000.

UU-8. Nobody checked whether the tutorials are internally consistent

Verifiers checked each claim against an external source. They did not systematically check claims against each other. Where they stumbled on an internal contradiction, they found real ones: quastel:1058 says the random walk hits the epigraph while :466, :475 and :1423 all say the hypograph; tardos asserts E[Q^r] ≤ C_r for every r ≥ 0 at :320 and :926 while its own Pemantle–Rosenthal statement at :619 gives only r < p−1; tolsa has cross-references that drift by one and two that point at sections which do not exist (§5.15, §9.2).

Three contradictions surfaced without anyone looking for them. That is a hit rate that suggests systematic checking would find more — and internal consistency is fully checkable with no papers at all, which makes it the cheapest unspent check available.

Done in round 2. verify/INTERNAL-CONSISTENCY.md. Cross-reference resolution over all 20 files found 14 broken internal § references in 4 files (gerard ×8, bartlett ×3, tolsa ×2, morris ×1), all from one cause: the back-matter section numbers differ per file and the references were written against another file's layout. Three of the four point at §12 or §13, which is wright's numbering. All seven cross-tutorial references resolve. The check also produced three named false-positive classes, recorded so nobody rebuilds the naive script.

UU-9. Exercise solutions were written by the same agent that set them

Every tutorial carries exercises with worked solutions. The one file where a verifier actually re-derived a solution — buffa — found the derivation wrong: :910 and :916 integrate ρ as a/δ after defining it as 1 + a/δ. The headline answer survived; the displayed map and the stated slope did not.

Solutions are the one part of a tutorial with no external source by construction — they are the writing agent's own work. So they are simultaneously the least verifiable part and the part a learner is most likely to check their own reasoning against.

Done in round 2 — all of them. verify/ROUND2-EXERCISES.md. 44 worked solutions across all 20 files, re-derived by hand. Result: zero wrong conclusions, and 9 local defects in 6 files — about 1 per 7 solutions. The single earlier data point (buffa, wrong) had made this look far worse than it is. Two things the buffa entry above did not say, now confirmed: its 34% headline is genuinely independent of the error, and the error does break the moral the exercise states, because the printed slope no longer equals the monitor ratio.

UU-10. The audit cost as much as it would cost to redo

This verification consumed about 2.08 million subagent tokens across 522 subagent tool calls. Re-verifying after the fixes land costs the same again. The original arXiv sweep cost 284,000 tokens on its own.

There is no cheap incremental re-check. Fixing gerard:665 and wanting confidence in it means re-running that file's verifier, roughly 127,000 tokens.

Do: batch the fixes, then re-verify once — never file by file. verify/BRIEF.md is saved so a re-run needs no new spec.
Round 2 revises the cost estimate sharply downward. It ran entirely in the main loop, with no subagents: 5 full independent verifications, 5 companion fetches, 44 exercise re-derivations and a corpus-wide consistency script, for roughly 450,000 tokens — about a fifth of round 1's 2.08M for work of comparable breadth. The saving is not cleverness; it is the absence of 15 agents each re-reading a 15,000-word file into its own fresh context.

12.2 The three findings that change what a reader should believe

A second MAJOR, in a file nobody had checked at this depth

random-interface-growth-quastel.md:481 prints the Brownian scattering operator with ℓ₁³ and ℓ₂³ in the exponents where Remenik's eq (4.2) has t∂³ in both. It is labelled "transcribed". §8.1b has the two lines side by side.

This is the exact prediction of UU-2 landing: a Grade B file, a dense-exponent subject, a formula that looks sourced because it names an equation number. It took about eight minutes to find once the paper was open.

The exercises are in far better shape than the one earlier sample suggested

UU-9 rested on a sample of one — the buffa solution, which was wrong. All 44 have now been worked by hand. None reaches a wrong conclusion. Nine carry a local defect: a constant, a sign, an index, or a pair of numbers that describe two different objects.

The interesting part is not the rate. It is that four of the nine are contradicted somewhere else in the same solution — mok states an exponent its own expansion disproves two lines above and its own part (c) disproves below; yau prints m′ = −m/(2m+z) = m/(m − 1/m), two expressions that differ by a sign; brendle states a metric and a curvature that belong to metrics differing by a factor of 4; ziegler says "at most 7 nonzero terms" one sentence after showing that three of the seven vanish.

A check that had started lying

verify/check_citations.py exited 1 and reported arXiv:2020.02998 as unresolved. It is unresolved — there is no month 20 — and that is the point: langlands-function-fields-gaitsgory.md:1548 quotes it in order to correct it, because it is the identifier Gaitsgory's own paper prints for [Zhu1]. HANDOFF.md said "exit 1 means a citation stopped resolving", which had become false.

A check whose failure state is permanent and benign is worse than no check: the next reader either chases a non-bug or learns to ignore the exit code. It now carries a one-entry allowlist with a comment pointing at the line, and exits 0.

12.3 What round 2 got wrong, or nearly did

  • Three false-positive classes in the consistency script, each of which would have been reported as a defect by a naive version. Bold inline subsection labels (bartlett writes §3.1–3.5 as **3.1 …**, not as headings) produced 6 false hits. A merged heading (manolescu's ### 4.4-4.5 Kirby diagrams) produced 1. And a per-line dollar-parity test produced about 60, because the files hard-wrap at 90 characters and inline maths straddles the break. Only whole-file parity means anything. All three are recorded in verify/INTERNAL-CONSISTENCY.md §3 so nobody rebuilds the naive script.
  • A near-miss on the yau constant. The stated c and the correct c agree exactly at E = 0, which is the value anyone would spot-check. Finding it needed the derivation, not a substitution.
  • The dolgopyat Poincaré map looked wrong and was not. The tutorial prints an indicator the survey prints differently — and the tutorial is right; the survey has a typo (χ_{[0,1/2)}(z) for (x)). The tutorial fixed it silently inside a passage introduced as a quotation. That is the correct mathematics and the wrong label.

12.4 What is still not checked, after both rounds

  • Nine files have no companion anybody can open. The SIAM proceedings chapters still return 403. arXiv:2412.20263 has no ar5iv or arXiv HTML rendering at all — only its abstract is readable. arXiv:2601.05425 (Manolescu) would not open in either round.
  • gaitsgory is the largest single exposure in the corpus. Its own source note says the captions carry not one formula, so every displayed statement in it comes from arXiv:2509.24902 — a paper nobody in this verification chain has read. Its round-2 report lists 16 numbered results resting on it.
  • The captions have still never been compared with the audio (UU-3), the field-status claims have still never been date-stamped (UU-6), and the timing metadata in CROSS-FILE.md §1 is untouched.
  • And the mathematics of the talks is still not audited. Round 2 checked what a source says against what a tutorial prints, and re-derived every exercise. Neither of those is peer review.

12.5 The thing worth carrying out of round 2

Round 1 spent 2.08 million subagent tokens and produced 15 reports. Round 2 spent roughly a fifth of that, in one context, and produced 5 more reports, 5 companion comparisons, 44 re-derivations and a corpus-wide script — plus the three findings above.

The difference is not model quality. It is that round 1's unit of work was a file and round 2's was a question. Fifteen agents each reading a 15,000-word tutorial into a fresh context is the expensive shape. "Do every exercise in the corpus" and "open every companion that resolves" are cheap shapes, because the reader stays warm and the comparison is local.

The corollary for the fix pass: do not re-run the tier 3 fan-out after applying the 35 ranked items. Re-run the specific checks the fixes touch, in one context, and re-run check_citations.py, which is free.
