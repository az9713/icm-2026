# ICM 2026 plenary lectures — 20 long-form tutorials

Twenty tutorials, one per plenary lecture of the International Congress of Mathematicians 2026,
written from the talks themselves. About **275,000 words**. Each one is a reading course, not a
summary: it builds the background a talk assumes, then walks the talk, then leaves worked
exercises with full solutions.

Source: the **[ICM 2026 Plenary Talks](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLFQq7_q5ORIs)**
playlist (Simons Foundation, 20 videos). Every tutorial's front matter carries its video URL, its
speaker, and the companion paper it was checked against, where one exists.

## Read this before you read a tutorial

**These were written for one reader.** They address a specific background — classical physics and
applied mathematics, no algebraic geometry, no algebraic topology, no number theory — in the second
person, and rate each talk against it. `TEMPLATE.md` is that specification. If your background is
different, the level ratings are the part that will not transfer.

**The talks were transcribed by machine, and machine captions carry no formulas.** Where a formula
was spoken but not recoverable, the tutorial says so in place — **34 `[Gap: …]` markers** across the
corpus — rather than guessing. That rule is the reason the documents are usable at all: a tutorial
with honest holes can be trusted around them; one with plausible fabrications cannot.

**They have been checked twice, and the defects found are recorded but not yet fixed.** See below.

§4 has the exact `yt-dlp` commands that regenerate them from the playlist.

## How far to trust these

The corpus was verified twice — once by fifteen independent agents reading one tutorial each, once
by a single reader asking one question at a time across all twenty. Both rounds recorded defects.
**Neither round edited a tutorial, and no fix has been applied since.**

What the checks establish:

- **No tutorial invented a theorem, and no tutorial fabricated a citation.** 100 arXiv IDs and 4
  DOIs all resolve (`verify/check_citations.py`, last run 2026-08-18).
- **Every title claim is correct** — 20 of 20. This was not free: one brief asserted a talk's
  subject from a video listing, and both the subject and its framing were wrong.
- **All 44 worked exercises have been re-derived by hand.** None reaches a wrong conclusion. Nine
  carry a local defect — a constant, a sign, an index.
- **Every companion paper a machine could reach has been read** and compared against the tutorial
  citing it. Formula errors turned up in 3 of the 11 files where that was possible.

What they do not establish:

- **Two files carry a MAJOR defect**, both a misquoted formula:
  `random-interface-growth-quastel.md:481` and `hardy-spaces-explicit-formulae-gerard.md:665`.
  Seventeen more carry MINOR ones. `verify/README.md` ranks all **35 open items**.
- **Nine tutorials have a companion nobody has read.** The largest exposure is
  `langlands-function-fields-gaitsgory`: its captions carry not one formula, so every displayed
  statement in it comes from `arXiv:2509.24902`, and 16 numbered results rest on that paper.
- **The mathematics of the talks is not audited.** Both rounds compared what a source *says*
  against what a tutorial *prints*. That is not peer review.

## Rendering

**Pre-rendered pages live in `html/`** — one per Markdown file, 48 of them, with a contents page at
`html/index.html`. They are self-contained apart from MathJax, which loads from a CDN, so open them
from disk or serve the directory. GitHub does not render HTML from a repository page; GitHub Pages
would.

The tutorials are Markdown with LaTeX — **367 `$$` display blocks and about 1,340 inline `$…$` spans**.
Six files use no LaTeX at all. A renderer without MathJax or KaTeX will produce two visibly
different classes of file. One known render defect is still open: `shape-of-math-kontorovich.md:637`
writes `**$10,000**` unescaped, which pairs with a later display block and swallows about 70 lines.
